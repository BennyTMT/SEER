**C. Prediction Knowledge**
1. **Regime Classification & Foundational Drivers**
Before forecasting any SKU, explicitly classify the regime (Squeeze vs. Glut) using a 4-point checklist to avoid catastrophic sign errors:
1. Is supply expanding (rising utilization/wafer output) or contracting?
2. Are downstream buyers accepting quoted price hikes or resisting/threatening non-purchase?
3. Is supplier guidance narrowing (loosening) or widening (tightening)?
4. Is the specific SKU trailing-edge (glut-prone) or leading-edge?
*Verdict:* If supply is contracting and buyers are accepting hikes (or "sold out" announcements occur), it is an UPSIDE squeeze. If supply is expanding and buyers resist, it is a DOWNSIDE cycle top.
*Corollaries:*
- CEO/manufacturer forward pricing language ("prices rising throughout the year") strongly overrides single-quarter analyst guidance.
- In a structural supply-deficit, price is set at the margin by supply scarcity; weak conventional demand does NOT cap prices.
- Spot market weakness (e.g., spot DDR4 drifting down) is transient retail noise; ignore it completely when forecasting CONTRACT SKUs.
§
2. **Execution Hierarchy & Math Discipline**
Forecast error is dominated by a strict precision hierarchy: (1) Correct regime sign, (2) Correct base selection, (3) Correct step timing (Q-boundary placement), and (4) Multiplier magnitude.
- *Target Math:* Compute the target strictly as: `Target = Correct_Base × Precedent_Multiplier`.
- *Rounding Discipline:* Carry one extra significant figure through the multiplication (e.g., Base × 1.780) before rounding the final target to two decimals. This shaves residual basis points and ensures cent-exact accuracy.
- *Error Propagation:* Base errors propagate multiplicatively. Base-inflation compounds magnitude misses: overshooting the pre-boundary base while simultaneously under-applying the boundary multiplier produces a catastrophic "double miss." Always anchor the structural leg to the correct SETTLED base.
- *Commitment:* Once the base, timing, and sign are locked, apply the SKU-specific realized multiplier with complete commitment. Do not artificially hedge, second-guess, or fine-tune to chase sub-0.3% precision.
§
3. **Base Calibration & Handling Guidance**
- *Settled Base:* If historical prints show the current quarter settled, carry that exact value forward flat to two decimals. Do NOT inflate it mid-quarter.
- *Synthesized Base from Guidance:* 
  - *Modest Guidance (e.g., 3-8%):* Settlements tend to clear near the arithmetic MIDPOINT.
  - *Demand Caveat:* If suppliers/analysts explicitly note demand "did not materialize as anticipated" or guidance was "narrowed," the settlement clears in the LOWER HALF of the range (e.g., ~4%), NOT the absolute minimum (~3%). Only push to the true floor when demand language is unambiguously weak AND supply is loosening.
- *Aggregate vs. SKU:* Do NOT convert an industry-aggregate contract-price % (e.g., TrendForce "13-18% Q2 DRAM increase") into a per-SKU price step. Aggregate figures blend many parts; the SKU's OWN historical prints already encode its settled move. Fabricating an intra-quarter lift to reconcile a SKU print with an aggregate headline is a critical failure mode.
§
4. **Structural Multipliers & Wafer Cannibalization**
Wafer reallocation to premium products creates violent, discontinuous price spikes. HBM consumes leading-edge wafers at a 3:1 bit ratio, starving both leading-edge and trailing-edge SKUs. Trailing-edge starvation often produces equal or greater price explosions due to severe capacity cuts and inelastic demand.
*First Full Structural Leg Multipliers:* Match the target SKU on node, capacity, and form-factor.
- Leading-edge DDR5 Server RDIMM: ~1.78x (Realized exactly 1.780x. Do not use the older ~1.807x ladder value for this SKU).
- Trailing-edge DDR4 32GB Module: ~1.845x
- Scarcest Trailing-edge DDR4 64GB RDIMM: ~1.880x
*Shock Amplification:* When a structural step is driven by a one-time physical shock (earthquake, fab outage) combined with a >20-25% manufacturer hike notice, the realized settlement (e.g., >78% jump) far EXCEEDS the nominal hike-notice %. The hike notice acts as a floor, and the physical shock amplifies it.
§
5. **Shape, Timing, and Plateau Discipline (Step-Then-Plateau)**
Contract-priced RAM SKUs move in discontinuous steps. The default shape is a flat base, ONE large discrete step, and then perfectly FLAT again.
- *Current Quarter Hold:* Hold the CURRENT quarter perfectly flat at the settled print. Even if your reasoning trace explicitly identifies an accelerating deficit ramping into the next quarter, acceleration expresses itself at the NEXT Q-boundary, not by retroactively inflating the already-settled current quarter.
- *Timing the Step:* Place the full structural step exactly at the FIRST month of the NEXT quarter (Q-boundary). The ABSENCE of published next-quarter analyst guidance is NEVER a valid reason to hold flat into an identified accelerating deficit. Structural squeezes reprice on physical scarcity, not on whether analysts have quantified a number.
- *Plateau Discipline:* Once a contract reprices, it holds perfectly flat. Never sprinkle small monthly gains ("marginal firmness") onto a plateau, never apply a reflexive decay tick, and ignore sub-0.2% micro-wobble within a settled quarter. One-time discrete physical shocks (earthquakes) ALWAYS produce a single step followed immediately by a flat plateau.
§
6. **Multi-Leg Squeezes & Leg-Counting Discipline**
Structural squeezes can arrive as multiple sequential legs, but you must strictly categorize prior steps to assign the correct multiplier.
- *Leg-Counting:* Modest single-digit intra-quarter steps (+3-8%) do NOT count as "structural legs." If the prior quarter merely settled at a mild step, the next Q-boundary jump is a FIRST full structural leg and clears at the ~1.78x - 1.88x floor (See Knowledge 4).
- *Second Leg Multiplier:* If the base already contains a violent structural spike (~1.8x), the NEXT structural leg at a later Q-boundary clears at the upper edge of the secondary band: ~1.43x.
- *Within-Quarter Staircase (Rare Exception):* Default shape is always Step-Then-Plateau. A high accelerant count of long-horizon narrative items (new fab CapEx, "sold out through 2025," GPU roadmaps) supports the FIRST leg and the plateau level—they do NOT generate fresh monthly steps. ONLY real-time worsening of a deficit within the quarter (rising HBM capacity share prints, sequential real-time hike notices) justifies a second within-quarter leg (~1.18x month-over-month). When uncertain, strongly prefer the plateau; over-forecasting multiple legs based on narrative backdrop is a primary failure mode.
